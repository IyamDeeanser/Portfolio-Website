Now building

Goblet

00  Overview

A regeneratively cooled flight engine.

Goblet is meant to be the first regeneratively cooled flight engine in Canada. It has to make 3.5 kN from a 3 inch chamber at an order of magnitude less mass than my previous engines, and survive multiple firings without damage.

3.5 kNthrust
1-2 kgtarget mass
01  Bulkhead

Where the vehicle meets the engine.

The bulkhead ties the thrust assembly to the vehicle and brings both propellant feeds down from the tanks. Everything directly above the engine was designed alongside it.

02  MOV and MFV

Main valves.

Servo driven Hylok valves control the main oxidizer and main fuel flow, mounted directly above the engine.

03  Chamber PT

Pressure transducer.

A pressure transducer has direct access to the chamber, measuring chamber pressure during every firing.

04  Igniter

Lit through the injector face.

A C6 motor fires straight through the injector face to light the engine.

05  Injector

Over 80 injector iterations.

The first six-element gas-centered coaxial swirl injector took four months of CFD and was hotfired in January 2026 at around 55% c* efficiency. Goblet now uses a paired like-doublet injector, which has also been printed.

80+CFD iterations
55%c* on first hotfire
06  Chamber

Printed in AlSi10Mg, cooled by its own fuel.

The chamber is printed in AlSi10Mg to fit a 5″ fincan. Fuel runs down the downcomer and back up a cooling jacket inside the walls before reaching the injector, which holds the liner around 300C, well below the alloy’s melting point.
